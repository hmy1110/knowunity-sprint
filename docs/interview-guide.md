# Interview guide: voice recall prototype

2 participants, about 20 minutes each. Mostly watching.

**Before you start:** open the prototype at the study plan home (`/`), 390px wide, mic permission already granted in the browser. Reload between tasks to reset.

## 1. Setup (say this out loud)

"This is a rough prototype. You're testing it, not me. There are no wrong moves. Please think out loud as you go, and I'll stay quiet."

"One thing to know: the speech part is mocked. The app isn't really listening, and it responds the same way whatever you say. Please talk as if it were real."

"Can I record the screen? It's fine to say no." (If no, take notes.)

## 2. Tasks

Read each one as a situation. Don't name any button or screen.

1. **"You've just finished revising Section 1 of your course and want to check whether you actually know it."**
   Watch: how they find the entry, what they expect it to do, whether they read the intro screen, where they hesitate before, during and after recording, what they do when the answer comes back.

2. **"You're on the bus and can't talk out loud, but you still want to do the check."** (Reload, start from home.)
   Watch: do they find the way to type, and how long does it take.

3. **"You've finished, and you want to know how you did and go over what wasn't solid."**
   Watch: what they read on the summary, whether they can say what it's telling them, what they reach for next.

Run one silent 30 seconds before helping. If they're stuck after that, ask "What are you thinking right now?" Never say where to tap.

## 3. Questions for afterwards

Ask about what they did, not what they think of it.

1. Go back to the [second / third / fourth] term. What happened there, and what did you do next? (Pick the term whose state you want to probe: 2 is the hint, 3 is the reveal, 4 is the skip.)
2. What did that last screen say about how you did? Show me where you got that from.
3. I noticed you paused at [moment from your notes]. What were you looking at?

## Note-taking

Just write down the time and the quote. Mark each note as **stuck**, **surprised**, **said something unprompted** or **misread**. Two participants show patterns, not numbers.

**Known confound:** the outcomes are scripted (term 1 recalled, 2 hinted, 3 revealed, 4 skipped). If someone says a correct answer and still gets a hint, that's a scripting artifact. But note their reaction anyway, because it's the "false wrong" the brief warns about.
